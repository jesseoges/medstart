🩺 SPECIALTY GUIDE

General Surgery

Operative care for abdominal, digestive, breast and soft-tissue conditions.

Common examples: Appendicitis, hernias, gallbladder disease, bowel obstruction.

Training overview

Medical degree and internship, then recognised surgery residency through the NPMCN and/or WACS route, subject to current requirements.

Detailed guide

Scope: Operative and peri-operative care for many abdominal, gastrointestinal, breast, endocrine and soft-tissue disorders. Work includes emergency assessment, elective surgery, surgical decision-making, complication prevention and follow-up.

Typical presentations: Appendicitis, hernias, gallstone disease, bowel obstruction, breast lumps and soft-tissue infections.

Training note: In Nigeria, after an accepted medical degree, internship and required registration/service obligations, doctors apply to an accredited residency programme. WACS Surgery commonly includes a Primary examination, approved junior rotations and Membership/Part I, followed by higher specialty training and Fellowship/Part II requirements. The exact sequence and duration depend on the current faculty curriculum and accredited centre.

🧠 SPECIALTY GUIDE

Neurosurgery

Surgery of the brain, spine and nervous system.

Common examples: Head injury, brain tumours, spinal compression, hydrocephalus.

Training overview

Medical degree and internship followed by competitive recognised neurosurgery residency. Confirm current NPMCN/WACS requirements.

Detailed guide

Scope: Surgical treatment of selected disorders of the brain, spine, spinal cord, peripheral nerves and their coverings. Decisions depend on neurological examination, imaging, urgency and multidisciplinary input.

Typical presentations: Head injury, intracranial haemorrhage, brain and spinal tumours, hydrocephalus, spinal compression and selected congenital disorders.

Training note: Usually follows medical school and internship, then recognised surgical residency with required examinations and rotations, followed by approved neurosurgical training. Entry is competitive and requirements must be confirmed with the current WACS Surgery curriculum and the training institution.

❤️ SPECIALTY GUIDE

Cardiothoracic Surgery

Surgery involving the heart, lungs and chest.

Common examples: Selected heart valve disease, lung tumours, chest trauma.

Training overview

Medical degree, internship, surgical training and recognised cardiothoracic subspecialty pathway; verify local programme requirements.

Detailed guide

Scope: Surgical care of selected diseases of the heart, lungs, oesophagus and other structures within the chest. It involves careful peri-operative planning and close work with cardiology, respiratory medicine, anaesthesia and intensive care.

Typical presentations: Selected valve or coronary disease, lung or mediastinal tumours, chest trauma and some congenital cardiac conditions.

Training note: The route generally requires a medical degree, internship, registration and an accredited surgical training pathway before focused cardiothoracic training. Exact rotations, examinations and duration are programme-specific; check the current WACS faculty curriculum.

🧸 SPECIALTY GUIDE

Pediatric Surgery

Surgical care for babies, children and adolescents.

Common examples: Congenital conditions, childhood hernias, appendicitis.

Training overview

Medical degree, internship, surgical residency and recognised paediatric surgery subspecialty training.

Detailed guide

Scope: Surgical care of neonates, infants, children and adolescents, taking account of age-specific anatomy, physiology, safeguarding and family-centred communication.

Typical presentations: Congenital anomalies, childhood hernias, appendicitis, intestinal obstruction, undescended testes and selected childhood tumours.

Training note: Typically requires medical school, internship and recognised surgical training before focused paediatric surgery training. Verify the current WACS curriculum and the accredited centre’s entry requirements.

🚑 SPECIALTY GUIDE

Trauma Surgery

Assessment and operative care for serious injuries in a coordinated trauma service.

Common examples: Major bleeding, chest injury, abdominal trauma, complex injuries.

Training overview

Build a surgical foundation and follow available recognised trauma training; pathways vary locally.

Detailed guide

Scope: Assessment and operative management of selected serious injuries as part of a coordinated trauma system. Priorities include structured assessment, haemorrhage recognition, resuscitation by trained teams and timely referral. Trauma surgery may overlap with general, orthopaedic, neurosurgical and cardiothoracic services.

Typical presentations: Blunt or penetrating abdominal injury, major bleeding, chest trauma, polytrauma and complications of serious injury.

Training note: “Trauma surgery” is not necessarily a separate direct-entry WACS faculty route. In Nigeria, doctors commonly build a recognised base in surgery or another relevant specialty and pursue trauma-focused experience or approved subspecialty training where available. Confirm the precise pathway locally.

🧵 SPECIALTY GUIDE

Plastic Surgery

Reconstructive and aesthetic procedures to restore function or appearance.

Common examples: Burns, wound reconstruction, congenital differences, tissue defects.

Training overview

Medical degree, internship and recognised plastic surgery residency; confirm current college requirements.

Detailed guide

Scope: Reconstructive surgery to restore function and form after injury, burns, cancer, congenital differences or tissue loss, as well as selected aesthetic procedures. Function, safety and realistic outcomes are central.

Typical presentations: Burns, complex wounds, skin and soft-tissue defects, hand injuries, congenital differences and reconstruction after tumour removal.

Training note: Usually follows medical school, internship and recognised surgical training, with the required examinations and focused plastic/reconstructive surgery training. Current WACS curriculum and programme accreditation determine eligibility.

🦴 SPECIALTY GUIDE

Orthopaedic Surgery

Care of bones, joints, ligaments, tendons and musculoskeletal injuries.

Common examples: Fractures, arthritis, sports injuries, limb deformities.

Training overview

Medical degree, internship and recognised orthopaedic residency.

Detailed guide

Scope: Prevention, diagnosis and treatment of disorders of bones, joints, ligaments, tendons, muscles and the musculoskeletal system. Care can include trauma, reconstruction, paediatric orthopaedics, spine and sports-related problems.

Typical presentations: Fractures, dislocations, osteoarthritis, ligament injuries, bone infection and limb deformity.

Training note: The WACS orthopaedic curriculum specifies approved rotations and examinations; requirements may include general surgery, emergency care, anaesthesia and elective exposure. The exact current curriculum and accredited programme should be checked before planning a route.

🎗️ SPECIALTY GUIDE

Surgical Oncology

Surgical management of solid tumours within a multidisciplinary cancer team.

Common examples: Breast tumours, colorectal cancer, selected soft-tissue tumours.

Training overview

Usually a surgical foundation followed by recognised oncology-focused training available at the chosen institution.

Detailed guide

Scope: Surgical management of selected cancers as part of a multidisciplinary team. Decisions depend on tumour type, stage, pathology, imaging, patient goals and whether surgery offers benefit alongside systemic therapy or radiotherapy.

Typical presentations: Breast, colorectal, endocrine and selected soft-tissue tumours, depending on local expertise and subspecialty practice.

Training note: Surgical oncology may be pursued through a recognised surgical foundation and further focused training; it is not necessarily a single uniform direct-entry WACS pathway across all institutions. Check the relevant faculty, recognised programme and current rules.

🤰 SPECIALTY GUIDE

Obstetrics and Gynaecology (OBGYN)

Care involving pregnancy, childbirth and reproductive health.

Common examples: Labour complications, ectopic pregnancy, fibroids, cervical screening.

Training overview

Medical degree, internship and recognised OBGYN residency.

Detailed guide

Scope: Care of pregnancy, labour, childbirth, reproductive health and disorders of the female reproductive tract. It combines medical care, procedures, prevention and emergency decision-making.

Typical presentations: Antenatal complications, labour problems, ectopic pregnancy, fibroids, abnormal uterine bleeding, infertility and cervical screening.

Training note: WACS has a dedicated Obstetrics & Gynaecology faculty. Following a recognised medical degree, internship, registration and applicable service obligations, candidates must meet the faculty’s current entry, training, logbook and examination requirements.

💓 SPECIALTY GUIDE

Cardiology

Diagnosis and non-surgical treatment of heart and circulation disorders.

Common examples: Hypertension, heart failure, rhythm disorders, coronary disease.

Training overview

Medical degree, internship, internal medicine training and recognised cardiology subspecialty training.

Detailed guide

Scope: Diagnosis, prevention and non-surgical treatment of cardiovascular disease, including risk-factor management, heart failure, rhythm disorders and coronary disease. Some procedures are performed by cardiologists with additional training.

Typical presentations: Chest discomfort, breathlessness, palpitations, syncope, hypertension and suspected heart failure.

Training note: Cardiology is physician-focused, so the relevant postgraduate pathway is generally through the West African College of Physicians (WACP) or applicable national college route, not WACS Surgery. Check current internal medicine and cardiology requirements.

🧠 SPECIALTY GUIDE

Neurology

Medical diagnosis and treatment of brain, spinal cord, nerve and muscle disorders.

Common examples: Stroke, epilepsy, migraine, neuropathy.

Training overview

Medical degree, internship and approved foundational training followed by recognised neurology training.

Detailed guide

Scope: Diagnosis and non-surgical management of disorders of the brain, spinal cord, peripheral nerves and muscles. Neurologists often coordinate long-term care and work with emergency, rehabilitation and surgical teams.

Typical presentations: Stroke, seizures, headache disorders, neuropathy, movement disorders and neuromuscular disease.

Training note: Neurology is generally pursued through physician training via WACP or the applicable national postgraduate college route, rather than the WACS surgical route. Confirm current eligibility and examination stages.

🧩 SPECIALTY GUIDE

Psychiatry

Medical care for mental, emotional and behavioural health.

Common examples: Depression, psychosis, bipolar disorder, substance-use disorders.

Training overview

Medical degree, internship and recognised psychiatry residency.

Detailed guide

Scope: Assessment and treatment of mental, emotional and behavioural disorders using biopsychosocial assessment, psychological interventions, medication when indicated and collaborative care.

Typical presentations: Mood disorders, psychosis, anxiety disorders, substance-use disorders and cognitive or behavioural changes.

Training note: Psychiatry is a physician specialty. Postgraduate entry and examinations are generally through WACP or the applicable national postgraduate college pathway. Current requirements should be verified with the relevant college.

⏱️ SPECIALTY GUIDE

Emergency Medicine

Initial assessment, stabilisation and treatment of urgent illness and injury.

Common examples: Severe asthma, sepsis, trauma, acute chest pain.

Training overview

Medical degree and internship, then a currently recognised emergency medicine training route where available.

Detailed guide

Scope: Rapid assessment, stabilisation and initial management of undifferentiated acute illness and injury, followed by appropriate disposition and coordination with specialty teams.

Typical presentations: Breathing difficulty, shock, stroke symptoms, severe infection, trauma and acute abdominal pain.

Training note: Emergency medicine training structures vary by country and institution. Do not assume that it is a standalone WACS surgical specialty. Confirm the recognised postgraduate route with the relevant college, regulator and accredited training centre.

🦠 SPECIALTY GUIDE

Infectious Diseases

Diagnosis and management of infections and antimicrobial decisions.

Common examples: Tuberculosis, HIV, severe infections, complicated malaria.

Training overview

Medical degree, internship, approved foundational training and recognised subspecialty route.

Detailed guide

Scope: Prevention, diagnosis and treatment of infections, antimicrobial stewardship, infection prevention and management of complex or unusual infections, including infections in immunocompromised patients.

Typical presentations: Persistent fever, severe infections, tuberculosis, HIV-related complications and complicated antimicrobial-resistant infections.

Training note: This is generally a physician-focused subspecialty following internal medicine training through WACP or the relevant national college route. Specific fellowship availability and entry requirements vary.

Paediatrics

Medical care for infants, children and adolescents.

Common examples: Pneumonia, childhood infections, growth concerns, neonatal illness.

Training overview

Medical degree, internship and recognised paediatrics residency.

Detailed guide

Scope: Medical care of newborns, children and adolescents, including growth, development, prevention, acute illness and chronic disease. Neonatal and paediatric subspecialties may require further training.

Training note: WACP has a paediatric training pathway. After a recognised medical degree and required internship/registration/service steps, candidates follow current college entry, training and examination rules.

🔬 SPECIALTY GUIDE

Pathology

Laboratory and tissue-based diagnosis that guides patient treatment.

Common examples: Cancer tissue diagnosis, blood disorders, infection testing.

Training overview

Medical degree and internship, then recognised pathology residency; laboratory-science routes differ by profession.

Detailed guide

Scope: Laboratory-based diagnosis of disease using tissue, cells, blood, microbiology and other investigations. Pathologists help interpret findings that guide treatment and public-health decisions.

Typical presentations: Tissue diagnosis of tumours, blood disorders, infection testing and correlation of laboratory findings with clinical information.

Training note: Medical pathology training routes and laboratory-science careers are not identical. Doctors should check the relevant WACP or WACS faculty and national postgraduate college rules for the specific pathology discipline.

🩻 SPECIALTY GUIDE

Radiology

Medical imaging and image-guided procedures for diagnosis and sometimes treatment.

Common examples: X-ray, CT/MRI, ultrasound and image-guided biopsy.

Training overview

Medical degree, internship and recognised radiology residency.

Detailed guide

Scope: Interpretation of medical images and, in some subspecialties, image-guided diagnostic or therapeutic procedures. Modalities include X-ray, ultrasound, CT, MRI and selected nuclear medicine techniques.

Typical presentations: Imaging for trauma, chest disease, abdominal pain, stroke, cancer staging and image-guided biopsy when indicated.

Training note: WACS has a Radiology faculty. Entry normally requires the recognised medical degree and required internship/registration/service steps, followed by the current faculty’s approved training and examinations.

🫘 SPECIALTY GUIDE

Urology

Surgical and medical care of the urinary tract and male reproductive system.

Common examples: Kidney stones, prostate conditions, urinary obstruction, urologic cancers.

Training overview

Medical degree, internship, surgical training and recognised urology training.

Detailed guide

Scope: Medical and surgical care of the urinary tract in all sexes and the male reproductive system. Practice includes endoscopic, open and minimally invasive procedures where available.

Typical presentations: Urinary stones, obstruction, prostate disorders, haematuria, urinary infections and urological cancers.

🌍 SPECIALTY GUIDE

Community Medicine

Population health, prevention, epidemiology and health-service improvement.

Common examples: Outbreak response, vaccination, screening and surveillance.

Training overview

Medical degree, internship and recognised community/public health medicine training.

Detailed guide

Scope: Improving health at population level through epidemiology, prevention, health promotion, surveillance, health services research and planning. The emphasis is on communities and systems as well as individuals.

Typical presentations: Outbreak investigation, vaccination planning, screening programmes, maternal-child health and evaluation of public-health interventions.

Training note: Public/community health postgraduate pathways vary by college and institution. Confirm whether the chosen programme is under WACP, a national postgraduate college or another recognised route; it is not automatically a WACS surgical route.

💉 SPECIALTY GUIDE

Anesthesiology

Perioperative care, anesthesia, pain management and vital-function support.

Common examples: Anesthesia for surgery, airway management, acute pain and perioperative stabilisation.

Training overview

Medical degree, internship and recognised anaesthesia residency.

Detailed guide

Scope: Peri-operative medical care including anaesthesia, airway and physiological support, pain management, resuscitation and aspects of critical care. Anaesthesiologists assess risk before procedures and monitor patients throughout recovery.

Typical presentations: Pre-operative assessment, anaesthesia for surgery, acute pain, airway challenges and peri-operative instability.

Training note: WACS has a dedicated Anaesthesiology faculty. Candidates must meet the current medical-degree, internship, registration/service, training and examination requirements for the accredited programme.

How the WACS route generally works

  1. Earn a recognised basic medical degree (such as MBBS/MBChB).
  2. Complete the required internship and obtain full registration with the national medical regulator. In Nigeria, check current MDCN requirements and NYSC or applicable service requirements.
  3. Pass the WACS Primary examination or obtain an approved exemption, then apply as a Surgeon-in-Training in an accredited programme.
  4. Complete the faculty's approved training, rotations, logbook and required courses, then sit the Membership/Part I examination when eligible.
  5. Complete further approved specialty training and requirements, then sit the Fellowship/Part II examination.
  6. For physician-focused specialties such as cardiology, neurology, paediatrics and infectious diseases, the relevant postgraduate route may be through the West African College of Physicians (WACP), not WACS. Confirm the appropriate faculty and entry route.

Important: Duration, entry sequence, accreditation, logbook numbers and examination rules differ by faculty and can change. This overview is not a guarantee of eligibility. Verify the current rules with the official WACS website and the relevant faculty curriculum.

Understanding the WACS postgraduate route

WACS is a postgraduate professional college, not an undergraduate medical school. The usual broad sequence is: recognised medical degree (MBBS/MBChB) → required internship and full registration with the national medical regulator → application for an accredited residency/training programme and registration with WACS as required → Primary examination in basic sciences where applicable → Membership/Part I examination after the prescribed training and documentation → Fellowship/Part II examination after further specialty training, logbook and other faculty requirements. Exact steps, durations, exemptions, course requirements and examination formats vary by faculty and current regulations.

Important: WACS does not run every specialty listed on this site as a separate faculty. For example, Cardiology, Neurology, Psychiatry, Paediatrics, Infectious Diseases and Community Medicine are generally physician/public-health pathways and should not be labelled as WACS surgical fellowships. Surgical subspecialties may require prior general surgical training or a post-fellowship programme. Anaesthesia, Obstetrics & Gynaecology, Orthopaedics and Radiology have their own WACS faculty routes. Always verify the exact current entry requirements and primary-exam syllabus on the relevant faculty page before planning.

Official reference: WACS official website · Faculty curricula and requirements · Faculty of Surgery curriculum.
