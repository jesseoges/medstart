Health Facts

Reliable health knowledge starts with evidence.

Vaccination

Vaccines train the immune system to recognise specific infections and can reduce severe disease and community spread. Follow the national immunisation schedule and advice from qualified health workers.

Hand hygiene

Washing hands with soap and safe running water helps reduce transmission of many infectious organisms, especially before food preparation and after using the toilet.

Antibiotics

Antibiotics treat susceptible bacterial infections, not viral illnesses such as uncomplicated colds. Use them only as prescribed and never share leftover antibiotics.

Blood pressure

High blood pressure often causes no obvious symptoms. Correct measurement and routine checks are important because untreated hypertension increases the risk of stroke, heart disease and kidney problems.

Heart attack warning signs

Chest pressure, breathlessness, sweating, nausea or discomfort in the arm, back, neck or jaw can be warning signs. Symptoms vary. Seek emergency medical care rather than waiting for them to pass.

Stroke warning signs

Sudden facial droop, weakness of an arm or leg, speech difficulty, vision loss or loss of balance needs urgent emergency assessment. Note when symptoms began.

Hydration

Fluid needs vary with age, activity, weather and illness. Diarrhoea and vomiting can cause dehydration; oral rehydration solution prepared exactly as directed can replace water and salts. Severe symptoms need medical care.

Sleep and learning

Regular, sufficient sleep supports attention, memory, mood and physical health. A consistent sleep schedule is usually more helpful than trying to compensate with irregular all-nighters.

Nutrition

A varied diet with vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains, protein sources and appropriate fluids supports health. Individual needs differ, and no single food can prevent or cure every disease.

Malaria prevention

In malaria-endemic areas, insecticide-treated nets, recommended vector-control measures and prompt testing for fever help reduce risk. Treatment should follow qualified local medical guidance.

HIV prevention and care

HIV can be prevented through evidence-based measures, and antiretroviral therapy allows people living with HIV to stay healthy. Testing and confidential care from qualified services are important.

Evidence matters

Look for guidance from recognised public-health agencies, professional medical bodies and peer-reviewed research. Be cautious of miracle cures, absolute promises and advice to stop prescribed treatment.

Get help when needed

Online information cannot replace assessment by a qualified health professional. Severe breathing difficulty, fainting, major bleeding, sudden neurological symptoms or severe chest pain require urgent help.
